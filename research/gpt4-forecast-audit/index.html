When a forecast gets the direction right, how much has it actually told us?

This paper audits archived GPT-4 forecasts against deliberately simple baselines, then asks whether forecast rankings improve choices among behavioral interventions once comparator identity and winner uncertainty are made explicit.

01 · The question

Direction is only the first rung of useful forecasting.

A sign score rewards an arbitrarily small positive forecast exactly as it rewards a well-calibrated positive forecast. But intervention choice often depends on magnitude, variation across contexts, and which option is best—not merely which way an average effect points.

03 · Matched-frame evidence

Perfect directional agreement concealed substantial differences in error.

The strict sample contains four topics crossed with anger, seriousness, and worry. Forecast values and randomized outcomes were hidden during the material-equivalence screen.

Contextual ordering

GPT-4 did not recover the observed ordering of the four topics: its largest forecasted topic mean corresponded to the smallest observed mean, while its smallest forecasted mean corresponded to the largest observed mean.

In this archive, recognizing a familiar direction was not evidence of incremental quantitative information.

Fixed-archive result

GPT-4 had the lowest posterior-mean regret in this fixed archive. Pairwise posterior comparisons, however, did not support lower regret for either equal-rank combination than for GPT-4 alone.

Winner-selection adjustment

Recomputing the latent feasible winner in every posterior draw raised GPT-4 regret from the observed-winner plug-in value of 0.651 to 0.755, tempering the most favorable plug-in result.

05 · Interpretation

What this audit does—and does not—establish.

The contribution is procedural: subtract achievements available to simple rules, identify the expert comparator precisely, and evaluate rankings against a noise-aware feasible oracle.
01

Fixed archive

The intervals condition on these archived forecasts, outcomes, and specified posterior models; they are not guarantees for future models or studies.

02

No human–AI synergy claim

Equal-rank combinations are analytic constructions, not observed expert deliberation or randomized advice uptake.

03

No welfare claim

The archive lacks intervention costs, an outside option, distributional consequences, and a social welfare function.

04

No autonomous policy claim

The analysis evaluates offline screening among archived arms, not whether any forecast-guided intervention should be deployed.

Directional accuracy is not the same as a useful forecast.
