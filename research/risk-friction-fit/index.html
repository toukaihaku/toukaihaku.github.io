Justification as a revisable state: a behavioral risk–friction fit framework for digital public services

A conceptual framework for deciding when a specific friction in a digital public service should be retained, redesigned, tested, suspended, or removed as evidence changes.

01 · The question

When does a protective step become unjustified friction?

Digital public services must control fraud, error, and consequential mistakes without excluding legitimate users. BRFF therefore asks whether one specified friction is justified for one specified risk, service stage, user group, and review period—not whether friction is good or bad in the abstract.

J(f, r, s, g, t | E, A)

Justification is conditional on the friction, risk, stage, group, time, evidence, and available alternatives.

01

Claimed protection

What risk is the step meant to reduce, and what incremental protection does it actually add?

02

Experienced burden

Who bears the time, uncertainty, cognitive demand, delay, or exclusion created by the design?

03

Revisable evidence

What is known, unknown, or no longer current—and when must the decision be reopened?

02 · Evidence-to-status framework

Separate design, mechanism, burden, outcome, and judgment.

BRFF prevents a stated purpose from being treated as proof. It records how a design is meant to work, how people experience it, what outcomes follow, and only then assigns a provisional status.
01

Design form

The concrete step, default, delay, document, channel, or interface being assessed.

02

Claimed mechanism

The behavior or information pathway through which protection is expected.

03

Experienced burden

The group-conditioned effort, uncertainty, delay, stigma, or access loss.

04

Behavioral outcomes

Protection-side and user-side consequences measured separately.

05

Normative status

A time-limited decision linked to evidence and an operational action.

The conditional gate sequence

G0

Authority & rights

Record legal authority and non-compensable rights floors before behavioral balancing begins.

G1

Functional fit

Test whether the friction adds protection for the specified risk, rather than merely appearing prudent.

G2

Comparative fit

Compare lower-burden alternatives across all material protection outcomes using prespecified loss margins.

G3

Distributional fit

Examine who gains, who bears the burden, and whether the current or candidate design respects access and rights floors.

i

BRFF is not a weighted score. Protection-loss margins apply only to protection outcomes; access and rights floors remain non-compensable.

03 · Revisable states

The same evidence does not lead symmetrically to every status.

Pass, Fail, and Unknown assessments map to four operational states. Unknown evidence cannot be silently converted into approval, while even a positive status expires and must be reassessed.
01

Justified for the Current Review Period

Required gates pass with adequate evidence.

Retain with monitoring, an expiry date, and a defined review trigger.
02

Evidence-Uncertain

Gate 0 passes, but a decision-relevant behavioral gate remains Unknown.

Suspend or defer by default. Provisional use requires severe target harm, at least Grade C support for every material protection outcome, safeguards, independent approval, evidence milestones, and expiry.
03

Correctable Misfit

Gate 1 passes and finalized Gate 2 or G3(f) fails, while an independently testable protective core and a feasible time-bounded repair remain.

Redesign, target, accommodate, or replace the failing delivery feature, then retest every affected gate.
04

Sludge

The friction fails protection, comparative, or distributional fit without a defensible route to correction.

Remove, suspend, or replace the friction and document the decision.
Justification is a time-indexed state—not a permanent label attached to an administrative requirement.

04 · Worked application

A measured access gain can reveal, rather than settle, the next policy question.

The paper applies BRFF to SNAP interview scheduling. The example shows how a published access result and an unmeasured integrity effect should be routed through the framework.
01

What is known

Flexible scheduling improved access and participation in the cited setting.

02

What remains unknown

The published record did not estimate whether fixed scheduling supplied incremental fraud or program-integrity protection.

03

What BRFF changes

The missing protection evidence becomes an explicit test, safeguard, milestone, and review deadline—not an assumption of no loss.

Interpretation boundary

This is an evidence-gap demonstration, not a new empirical estimate. The two completed records in the article use hypothetical values and do not validate the framework.

05 · Scope and limits

An auditable decision structure, not an automated decision rule.

BRFF organizes the consequences of incomplete evidence. It does not remove the need for legal judgment, institutional capacity, measurement, or public accountability.
01

Conceptual synthesis

The article is not a systematic review or meta-analysis and does not estimate a pooled effect.

02

No validated scale

BRFF is not a psychometric instrument, causal model, or mechanically weighted index.

03

Illustrations are not evidence

The worked records are deliberately hypothetical and should not be read as empirical validation.

04

Implementation matters

Evidence quality, monitoring, expiry rules, and the ability to change service design determine whether the framework can discipline practice.

Purpose cannot substitute for performance; unknown cannot become approval; positive status cannot persist without renewal.
